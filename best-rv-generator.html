Best RV Generators 2026: Quiet Power That Doesn't Annoy Your Neighbors

📖 10 min read Updated: May 2026

There's a special kind of hostility reserved for the camper who fires up a loud contractor generator at 7 AM. Don't be that person. A good RV generator runs quiet, sips fuel, and gives you enough juice to run your AC without tripping breakers. Here's what actually works.

Inverter vs Conventional: Why It Matters

Inverter generators are what you want for RVing. They produce clean, stable power that's safe for laptops and phones. They throttle up and down based on load, so they're quieter and use less fuel. Most RV-friendly generators are inverter types.

Conventional generators run at full speed constantly. They're louder, thirstier, and the power isn't clean enough for sensitive electronics. Fine for job sites. Terrible for campgrounds.

💡 Key Number

To run a 13,500 BTU RV air conditioner, you need at least 2,200W starting and 2,000W running. Smaller generators won't cut it for AC. Know this before you buy.

Generator vs Solar vs Power Station

Quick comparison for the "should I even get a generator?" crowd:

Generator: Best for extended off-grid stays. Unlimited runtime as long as you have fuel. Runs AC. Downside: noise, fumes, maintenance.

Solar: Silent, free fuel (sunshine), zero maintenance. But needs good sun and a big battery bank. Can't run AC without a massive system ($5,000+).

Power station (Jackery/EcoFlow): Silent, plug-and-play, no gas needed. Great for 1–2 nights off-grid. Not enough for extended stays or AC. More of a supplement than a primary power source.

💡 Our Take

Solar + battery for everyday power. Generator as backup for cloudy days and running AC. Power station for short trips. That's the trifecta.

Fuel Types: Gas, Propane, or Dual-Fuel?

Gas is available everywhere but goes stale in 3–6 months. Not ideal if you store the generator months between uses.

Propane stores indefinitely, burns cleaner, and you probably already have tanks for your RV. Slightly less power output than gas.

Dual-fuel gives you both options. Champion makes several dual-fuel models that run on either. The flexibility is worth the small premium.

The Safety Stuff Nobody Talks About

  • Never run a generator inside your RV. Carbon monoxide kills. Not "might" kill—kills.
  • Keep it at least 10 feet from any window or door, pointing the exhaust away.
  • Use a heavy-duty extension cord rated for outdoor use. Cheap cords melt.
  • Most campgrounds have quiet hours (8 PM – 8 AM). Plan your generator use around them.
  • Some national parks ban generators entirely. Check before you go.
