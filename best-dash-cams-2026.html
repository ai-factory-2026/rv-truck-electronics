Key Features That Matter for Truckers

Before you buy, make sure your dash cam has these essentials:

Temperature Resistance

Your truck baking in an Arizona sun or sitting in a frozen Minnesota rest stop will kill cheap electronics. Look for cameras with supercapacitors instead of lithium batteries—they handle temperature extremes much better.

Parking Mode

This is non-negotiable if you park overnight at truck stops. Motion-activated recording catches hit-and-runs, vandalism, and thieves. You'll need a hardwire kit or constant power source to make this work.

Loop Recording

Your camera should automatically overwrite old footage when storage fills up. Look for configurable loop intervals (1, 3, 5 minutes) so you can preserve important clips before they get overwritten.

GPS Logging

Location and speed data can be crucial in accidents. Even if you're not at fault, having proof of your exact location and speed helps clear up disputes.

High Storage Capacity

Get at least 128GB. For 2-channel systems, 256GB is better. Trust me—you don't want to be the guy who "should have gotten more storage" right before something happens.

Installation Tips

Most dash cams run off a 12V power outlet (cigarette lighter). This is fine for testing, but for permanent use, hardwire it:

  • Use a compatible hardwire kit with voltage monitoring
  • Connect to a constant and ignition-switched fuse
  • Route cables cleanly along the headliner and A-pillar
  • For parking mode, add a battery pack if available

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  1. Buying based on Amazon reviews alone — Some cameras have 10,000 reviews but are garbage. Look at long-term reviews.
  2. Skimping on storage — 64GB is not enough. Trust me.
  3. Ignoring mounting options — Some cameras use adhesive, others use suction. Adhesive is generally more secure for trucks.
  4. Forgetting about the rear camera — Front-only coverage leaves you blind to rear impacts, which are common in parking lots.

FAQ: Truck Dash Cams

What is the best dash cam for semi truck drivers?

After testing the top dash cams, the Thinkware U1000 is our pick for semi truck drivers due to its 4K front recording, cloud connectivity, and solid build quality that withstands truck vibrations.

Do truck dash cams record at night?

Yes, most quality dash cams record at night using infrared LEDs or low-light sensors. The BlackVue DR900X and Thinkware U1000 excel in night recording quality.

Can I use a regular dash cam in a semi truck?

Regular dash cams can work in trucks, but truck-specific models handle higher vibrations, have longer power cables, and often include cabin or side-facing cameras.

How much storage do I need for dash cam footage?

For 24/7 recording, we recommend at least 128GB minimum. A 256GB card in a 2-channel 1080p system gives you about 30-40 hours of footage before loop recording overwrites.
