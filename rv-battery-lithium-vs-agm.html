Battery Types Explained

Lead-Acid (Flooded)

The old-school battery you grew up with. Requires maintenance: adding distilled water, cleaning corrosion, equalizing charges. Cheap upfront, but high maintenance and short lifespan.

Best for: Occasional weekend campers on a tight budget who don't mind maintenance.

AGM (Absorbed Glass Mat)

Sealed lead-acid battery. No maintenance, better performance, faster charging. More expensive than flooded but cheaper than lithium. Handles moderate deep cycling well.

Best for: Casual to moderate boondockers who want sealed battery simplicity.

Lithium (LiFePO4)

The new standard for serious off-grid RVers. Lithium iron phosphate chemistry is safe, stable, and delivers way better performance than lead-acid. High upfront cost, but pays off over time.

Best for: Full-time RVers, boondockers, and anyone who wants the best performance.

💡 The Real Cost Math

A $1,000 lithium battery lasting 10 years might cost you $100/year. A $150 lead-acid battery needing replacement every 2 years costs $75/year — but you'll replace it 5 times and deal with 5x the maintenance headaches. Lithium often works out cheaper long-term for serious users.

Which Battery for Your Camping Style?

Weekend Warriors (10-20 nights/year)

Recommendation: AGM or budget lithium

You're plugged in at the campground most of the time. You just need reliable house power. AGM is fine — the maintenance isn't too bad with limited use, and you won't stress-test the battery enough to need lithium's advantages.

Seasonal Campers (50-100 nights/year)

Recommendation: AGM or mid-range lithium

You're dry camping occasionally but mostly at full-hookup sites. AGM works fine if you're diligent about maintenance. If you can afford the upgrade, lithium makes boondocking much easier with faster charging and no maintenance.

Full-Timers & Boondockers

Recommendation: Lithium, no question

You live in your RV or regularly camp without hookups. Lithium's fast charging, full depth-of-discharge, and long life are essential. You'll recover from solar faster, last longer between generator runs, and never worry about battery maintenance. The extra cost pays for itself in 2-3 years.

Winter RVers

Recommendation: Lithium with cold-charging protection

Cold weather is tough on batteries. Lithium can be damaged by charging below freezing — make sure your lithium battery has built-in cold charging protection (most quality ones do). AGM handles cold better than standard lithium but not as well as heated options.

⚠️ Important: Never charge any lead-acid battery below 32°F — it can freeze and crack the case. If you're winter camping, keep batteries insulated and consider lithium with cold charging protection.

Sizing Your Battery Bank

How much battery do you actually need? Here's the real-world formula:

Step 1: Calculate Daily Usage

Add up what you use (in amp-hours):

  • LED lights: 1-2 Ah/night
  • Fantastic fan: 5-20 Ah/night
  • Water pump: 3-5 Ah/night
  • Fridge (propane/electric): 5-15 Ah/night
  • Inverter losses: +20%

Step 2: Add It Up

Typical boondocking setup: 60-80 Ah/day

Minimal setup: 30-40 Ah/day

Heavy use (air conditioning, etc.): 100-200 Ah/day

Step 3: Size for 2-3 Days Autonomy

You want enough battery to survive 2-3 cloudy/rainy days without solar charging:

  • 60 Ah/day × 2 days = 120 Ah minimum
  • With lead-acid (50% max discharge): 120 ÷ 0.5 = 240 Ah battery bank
  • With lithium (100% usable): 120 ÷ 1.0 = 120 Ah battery bank

💡 Pro Tip: Don't Cheap Out

Battery banks are not the place to save money. Under-sizing leads to: sulfation (permanent battery damage), shortened lifespan, and being stuck without power. Size for your worst-case scenario (multiple cloudy days, heavy use) and you'll thank yourself later.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I mix battery types in my RV?

No. Never mix different battery types (lead-acid with lithium, AGM with flooded, etc.) or different ages/capacities. This causes imbalances, reduces lifespan, and can be dangerous. Keep all batteries in a bank identical.

Do I need a special charger for lithium batteries?

Yes. Lithium batteries need a charger that outputs 14.2-14.6V (LiFePO4 profile). Most modern converters/inverters have lithium settings. If yours doesn't, you'll need to upgrade the charger. Using a lead-acid charger on lithium can damage it or cause safety issues.

Can I use my existing solar panels with lithium batteries?

Yes, and lithium actually charges from solar more efficiently than lead-acid. Lithium can accept higher charging current, so you can use your existing panels and they'll charge faster. Just make sure your solar controller has a lithium setting.

How do I know when to replace my batteries?

Signs of failing batteries: visible swelling, corrosion that won't clean off, voltage won't hold (drops quickly under load), takes forever to charge, or the capacity is noticeably lower than when new. If your batteries are more than 5-7 years old, start budgeting for replacement.

Is it worth upgrading from lead-acid to lithium?

If you boondock regularly or are frustrated with lead-acid maintenance, yes. The quality-of-life improvement is significant: no more checking water levels, faster charging, using your full battery capacity, lighter weight. Calculate your usage and see if the numbers work — for full-timers or heavy boondockers, lithium almost always wins.
