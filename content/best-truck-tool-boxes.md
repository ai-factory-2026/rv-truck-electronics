# Best Truck Tool Boxes: Secure Storage That Actually Fits

A good truck tool box is the difference between gear that's organized and protected, and gear that's rolling around your truck bed getting destroyed by weather and theft. After researching 20+ models and talking to contractors who use them daily, I've narrowed down the best truck tool boxes for every budget and truck size. Here's what actually holds up.

## Types of Truck Tool Boxes

### Crossover (Saddle) Boxes
The most popular type. Sits across the truck bed right behind the cab. Access from the top, doesn't take up bed floor space.

**Best for:** Most truck owners, daily tool access, maximizing bed space

### Side-Mount Boxes
Mounted on the side rails of the truck bed. Keeps the full bed length available.

**Best for:** Long cargo that needs the full bed, contractors who need quick side access

### Chest Boxes
Sits on the bed floor at the front. Lid opens from the top. Maximum storage volume.

**Best for:** Maximum storage capacity, heavy-duty use, off-road trucks

### Undercover/Under-Rail Boxes
Mounted below the bed rail, invisible from outside. Great for a clean look with hidden storage.

**Best for:** Daily drivers who want secure hidden storage, maintaining a stock appearance

## How to Choose the Right Size

Measure your truck bed's inside width at the top of the rails before ordering:

| Truck | Bed Width (Inside Rails) | Recommended Box Width |
|-------|--------------------------|----------------------|
| Chevy Silverado (short bed) | 63.5" | 63-63.5" |
| Ford F-150 (short bed) | 63.4" | 63-63.5" |
| Ram 1500 (short bed) | 63.2" | 63" |
| Toyota Tundra (short bed) | 63.2" | 63" |
| Chevy Colorado (mid-size) | 55.5" | 54-55" |
| Toyota Tacoma (mid-size) | 55.8" | 54-55" |

**Rule of thumb:** Your box should be 1-2 inches narrower than your bed's inside width for a proper fit.

## Installation Tips

### Crossover Box Installation

1. **Place the box** in the bed, centered behind the cab
2. **Mark drill points** through the mounting holes onto the bed rail
3. **Drill pilot holes** (use a bit one size smaller than your bolts)
4. **Apply silicone sealant** to each hole to prevent rust
5. **Bolt the box down** using the included hardware
6. **Check the lid alignment** — adjust strikers if needed

**Time:** 30-45 minutes with basic tools (drill, socket set, measuring tape)

**Pro tip:** Don't overtighten the bolts on aluminum boxes. The metal can deform, creating gaps in the weather seal. Snug is enough.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Will a crossover box work with a tonneau cover?
It depends on both the box and the cover. Low-profile crossover boxes work with most folding tonneau covers. Measure from the bed rail to the top of the box — if it's under 8 inches, most covers will clear it.

### Can I install a tool box without drilling?
Some boxes use J-hooks or C-clamps that grip the bed rail without drilling. These work but are less secure and can shift over time. For permanent installation, drilling is recommended.

### How much weight can a truck tool box hold?
Most crossover boxes are rated for 200-400 lbs. The limiting factor is usually the gas struts (they struggle with very heavy loads) rather than the box itself. Check the manufacturer's weight rating.

### Should I get aluminum or steel?
For 95% of truck owners, aluminum is the right choice. It's lighter, won't rust, and is plenty strong. Steel is only worth considering if you need maximum security or work in extreme environments where the box takes physical abuse.
