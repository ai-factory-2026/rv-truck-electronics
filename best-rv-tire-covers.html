Why RV Tires Die From Sitting

RV tires are different from car tires. Car tires get replaced every 40–50K miles from wear. RV tires often die from age before the tread wears out, because RVs sit for months at a time.

The sun's UV rays break down the rubber compound, causing sidewall cracking (called "weather checking" or "dry rot"). Once those cracks appear, the tire is compromised. No fix—just replacement.

The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration recommends replacing RV tires every 5–7 years regardless of tread depth. With UV protection? You might squeeze 7–10 years. Without it? 3–5 years before cracking starts.

💡 The Other Killer

UV gets the spotlight, but ground moisture is just as bad. Tires sitting on damp ground absorb water from the inside of the wheel, leading to rim corrosion and rubber degradation. Good tire covers block moisture too.

How to Size Tire Covers Correctly

Measure the tire diameter, not the wheel diameter. A 16-inch wheel might have a tire that's 30–33 inches in diameter. Check your tire sidewall for the size (e.g., 225/75R15) and use a tire size chart to find the diameter.

Common RV tire sizes and their cover sizes:

When in doubt, size up. A slightly loose cover is better than one that stretches tight and stresses the elastic.

Other Tire Protection Tips

  • Park on boards or pads, not bare ground or asphalt. Concrete is okay short-term but still wicks moisture.
  • Inflate to max sidewall pressure before storing. Under-inflated tires flat-spot faster.
  • Move the rig every 2–3 months if possible. Even rolling it a few feet prevents flat spots.
  • Check tire pressure monthly during storage. Slow leaks are normal—catch them before the tire goes flat.
  • Cover all tires, including the inside duals. The sun reaches them too.
