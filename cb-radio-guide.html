Sure, we've got smartphones and dispatch apps now. But CB radio? It's instant, free, and works where cell service doesn't. Let me break down what you need to know.

CB Radio Basics

Citizens Band (CB) radio operates on 40 channels between 26.965-27.405 MHz. No license required, no subscription, just turn it on and talk.

The Channels

  • Channel 19: The trucking channel. This is where you'll find most drivers.
  • Channel 9: Emergency channel. Keep this programmed for quick access.
  • Channel 19 again: Yes, I'm serious. It's trucking chatter central.
  • Channels 1-18: Various uses, some local chatter.
  • Channels 20-40: Less commonly used but available.

Real-World Range

Ignore the 100-mile marketing claims. Realistically:

  • Flat terrain, good antenna: 5-10 miles
  • Mountains/urban: 1-3 miles
  • Ideal conditions: Maybe 15-20 miles (rare)

Your antenna matters more than your radio. A cheap radio with a great antenna beats a great radio with a cheap antenna every time.

What to Look For

Power Output

Legal max is 4 watts AM. Some radios advertise higher power with SSB (up to 12 watts). More power = more range, but antenna quality matters more.

SWR Meter

Standing Wave Ratio. You need SWR under 2:1, ideally below 1.5:1. Higher SWR means reflected power and potential radio damage. Built-in meters are nice; standalone meters are better.

PA Capability

Public Address mode uses your CB mic and a speaker to broadcast externally. Nice for communicating with loaders or shippers when you're backing in.

Weather Channels

Some CB radios have built-in NOAA weather radio. This is genuinely useful for storm tracking.

Installation Guide

Mounting

  • Under-dash bracket: Classic approach, keeps radio visible but out of the way.
  • Console mount: More visible, easier to reach.
  • Headliner mount: For compact radios or if you want it overhead.

Power

Wire directly to your truck's battery. Fused positive lead, ground to chassis. Never use the cigarette lighter—too much noise and interference.

Antenna Tuning

CRITICAL: After installation, tune your antenna with an SWR meter. Adjust length until SWR is below 2:1 on all 40 channels. This is non-negotiable.

CB Radio Etiquette

  • Identify yourself occasionally ("This is Big Red, checking in")
  • Don't hog channel 19
  • Keep language clean on channel 9 (emergency channel)
  • Use plain English, not codes unless you know the other party
  • Don't transmit without identifying yourself
  • Be helpful to other drivers

Common Mistakes

  1. Cheap antenna: Spend $50-80 on a good antenna, not $30 on a junk one.
  2. No SWR tuning: You'll damage your radio and have poor performance.
  3. Wrong mount placement: Antenna needs a good ground plane. Mirror mounts work best.
  4. Overmodulation: Don't crank up the power beyond legal limits. It sounds bad and causes interference.
  5. Ignoring local laws: Some areas restrict CB usage. Know the rules.

Do I really need a CB radio in 2026?

While not required, a CB radio is still valuable for truckers. It provides instant communication about traffic, accidents, weigh station status, and road conditions. It works in areas with no cell service.

What is the best CB radio brand?

Cobra and Uniden are the two dominant CB radio brands. Cobra has better warranty and customer service, while Uniden often offers more features for the price.

Can a CB radio reach 100 miles?

Realistically, CB radio range is 1-10 miles depending on terrain and conditions. In ideal conditions with a good antenna, you might get 20 miles. Claims of 100+ miles are marketing hype.

How much does it cost to install a CB radio?

Budget $100-300 total: $50-150 for the radio, $30-80 for antenna/coax, $20-40 for mounts. Professional installation adds $100-200.
