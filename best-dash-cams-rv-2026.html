RV Dash Cam Buying Guide

Not all dash cams work on RVs. Here's what actually matters for larger vehicles:

1. Voltage & Power

RVs run on 12V systems (or 24V for some diesel pushers). Most dash cams accept 12-24V input. Check before buying — some cheaper models only work on 12V and will fry on a 24V coach.

2. Mounting Options

RVs have larger windshields and often more curved glass than cars. Look for:

  • Wide-angle lenses (150°+ for front, 160°+ for rear)
  • Adhesive mounts (more secure than suction for RVs)
  • Extension cables for rear camera (often 20-30ft needed)

3. Temperature Rating

RVs bake in the summer sun. Your dash cam will be sitting in 150°F+ heat on the dashboard. This kills cheap cameras. Look for:

  • Operating temp: -20°F to 150°F minimum
  • Supercapacitor instead of lithium battery (more heat stable)
  • Parked car recording capability (won't cook the battery)

4. Video Quality

You need to read license plates, especially at night. Skip anything under 1080p — 4K is ideal but 1440p is fine.

  • Minimum: 1080p front, 1080p rear
  • Recommended: 4K front, 1080p rear
  • Night vision: Sony Starvis sensor or similar
⚠️ RV-Specific Issue: If your RV has a rain-sensor windshield with a tinted strip at the top, the camera might not mount properly. Check the mount dimensions before ordering.

Backup Cameras for Travel Trailers & Fifth Wheels

Trailers have different needs than motorhomes — you need to see behind the trailer, not the tow vehicle. Here are the options:

Wired Systems

Most reliable but requires running wires from tow vehicle to trailer. Furrion and Voyager are the standards at RV parks — most campground hookups have Furrion mounting brackets pre-installed.

Wireless Systems

Easier to install but can have interference issues. RVs with multiple wireless devices (campground WiFi, cell boosters, etc.) sometimes cause signal dropout.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use a car dash cam on my RV?

Yes, but check voltage compatibility (12V vs 24V), temperature ratings, and make sure the angle is wide enough for a larger windshield. Most car dash cams work fine on Class B and C RVs.

Do dash cams work when parked?

Yes, if you have a dash cam with parking mode. It records when motion is detected (or continuously at lower bitrate). Important for campground security. Note: In hot weather, lithium battery cameras can overheat — use supercapacitor models for parked recording in hot climates.

How do I hardwire a dash cam to an RV?

Use a hardwire kit connected to the fuse box. RVs often have constant (battery) and switched (ignition) fuses. Connect to the switched fuse for recording while driving, or constant for parking mode (requires low-voltage cutoff to prevent battery drain). Hardwire kits run $15-30.

What's the best dash cam for boondocking?

Look for: parking mode with motion detection (saves battery), solar-friendly power draw, supercapacitor (heat safe), and WiFi for video review without running the TV/monitor. The Thinkware U1000 or BlackVue DR900X are top picks for boondockers.

Can dash cam footage be used as evidence?

Yes, absolutely. Dash cam footage is routinely accepted in court and insurance disputes. It should clearly show: the other vehicle, the incident, and ideally the other driver's face or license plate. Make sure your camera has GPS overlay showing speed and location — this strengthens evidence.
