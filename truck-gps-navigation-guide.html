Why You Need Truck-Specific GPS

Consumer GPS apps and units are built for cars. When you're driving an RV or semi, you'll run into problems:

  • Low bridges: Google Maps doesn't know you're 13'6" tall
  • Weight limits: Road restrictions for heavy vehicles
  • Hazmat routes: Some roads don't allow hazardous materials
  • Truck stops: Consumer apps don't prioritize truck-friendly stops
  • Turn restrictions: Semis can't make sharp turns
  • Fuel optimization: Finding diesel is different than regular gas

A truck GPS knows your dimensions and routes you accordingly. One stuck-under-a-bridge incident costs more than any GPS.

Best Garmin Dezl Units

Garmin dominates the truck GPS market. The Dezl series is their dedicated truck line.

Garmin Dezl OTR800 (Top Pick)

Price: $500-600 | Screen: 8" | Maps: US + Canada

The flagship truck GPS. Giant 8" screen is easy to read at a glance. Preloaded with truck-specific maps, weight limits, bridge heights, and hazmat restrictions. Loads routes for your truck's specs. Includes dēzl app for smartphone notifications.

Pros: Huge screen, excellent routing, wifi updates, good voice commands

Cons: Expensive, app could be better

Garmin Dezl OTR700 (Best Value)

Price: $350-450 | Screen: 7" | Maps: US + Canada

Seven-inch version of the OTR800. Same routing intelligence, slightly smaller screen. If you can live without the extra inch, you save $150.

Pros: Great routing, solid screen size, good value

Cons: Smaller screen than OTR800

Garmin Dezl LGV1000 (Luxury Option)

Price: $800-900 | Screen: 10" | Maps: US + Canada

Ten-inch display for big rigs. Same routing as OTR series but in an even larger format. If you have a commercial cab with mounting space, this is the ultimate cockpit upgrade.

Pros: Huge screen, excellent routing

Cons: Very expensive, overkill for casual use

Best Rand McNally Units

Rand McNally has been making truck maps for decades. Their GPS units are solid alternatives to Garmin.

Rand McNally OverDryve 8 Pro (Top Pick)

Price: $400-500 | Screen: 8" | Maps: US + Canada

Rand McNally's answer to Garmin Dezl. 8" screen, truck-specific routing, and includes a built-in dash cam. The cam isn't best-in-class but it's integrated, which is nice. Rand McNally's trip planning is excellent.

Pros: Built-in dash cam, good trip planning, solid routing

Cons: Some reliability issues reported, UI can be clunky

Rand McNally TND 540 (Budget)

Price: $250-350 | Screen: 5" | Maps: US

Basic truck GPS at a budget price. 5" screen is small for a big rig but gets the job done. Still has truck routing, weigh stations, and fuel prices. Good for part-time truckers or backup unit.

Pros: Affordable, truck routing works

Cons: Small screen, basic features

Truck GPS Apps

Dedicated GPS units are great, but apps give you flexibility. Here are the best truck routing apps:

CoPilot (Best Truck App)

Price: $100/year or $300 lifetime | Platform: iOS/Android

CoPilot is the only mainstream app with full truck routing. Enter your dimensions, it routes accordingly. Works offline (download maps). The premium subscription adds real-time traffic and truck stops.

Google Maps (Limited Use)

Price: Free | Platform: iOS/Android

Google Maps now has truck routing — but it's basic. Set your vehicle type and dimensions, and it will try to route around restrictions. It's better than nothing but not as thorough as dedicated truck GPS units.

Good for: Casual RVs, verifying routes, backup navigation

Bad for: Commercial trucking, full-time rig life

Waze

Price: Free | Platform: iOS/Android

Waze doesn't have truck routing, but it's excellent for real-time traffic, hazards, and police. Use it alongside your truck GPS for extra awareness. The crowdsourced data is unmatched.

Key Features to Look For

Not all truck GPS units are equal. Here's what matters:

Truck Routing Parameters

  • Height, width, length limits
  • Weight restrictions (axle and gross)
  • Hazmat route compliance
  • Steep grade warnings
  • Sharp curve warnings

Truck Amenities

  • Truck stop database with amenities
  • Diesel fuel prices
  • Weigh station bypass info
  • Rest areas
  • RV parks (for RVs)

Connectivity

  • WiFi map updates (vs. computer required)
  • Bluetooth for calls
  • Smartphone integration
  • Backup camera input

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use Google Maps for my RV?

For Class B and C RVs under standard height limits, Google Maps works okay. Set your vehicle type in settings. For Class A RVs or any vehicle over standard dimensions, get a dedicated truck GPS or CoPilot app.

Do truck GPS units require subscriptions?

Garmin and Rand McNally don't require annual subscriptions — you buy the unit and get map updates included. CoPilot app requires either annual subscription or one-time purchase. After initial purchase, there are no required fees.

How often do I need to update maps?

At least annually. Roads change constantly — new roads, closed roads, updated restrictions. Most modern units update via WiFi in a few minutes. Don't skip updates, especially before long trips.

What's the best GPS for a sprinter van?

Sprinter vans are tricky — they're tall (9'+ with roof rack) but not as long/wide as semis. A 7" truck GPS works fine, or use CoPilot app with Sprinter van settings. Garmin Dezl OTR700 is a good choice.

Should I get a dash cam with my GPS?

Yes, if you're serious about protecting yourself. Dash cams are cheap insurance for accidents that aren't your fault. The Rand McNally OverDryve has one built in, or get a separate unit like the Garmin Dash Cam series.
