RV Leveling Jacks & Stabilizers 2026: Stop the Wiggle

📖 8 min read Updated: May 2026

Nothing kills the RV vibe faster than feeling every footstep. Your partner gets up for water at 2 AM and the whole rig sways like a boat. The fix isn't complicated—you just need the right jacks and a few tricks that most RV dealers won't mention.

Leveling vs Stabilizing: Not the Same Thing

This trips up a lot of new RV owners:

Leveling jacks lift corners of the RV to make it level side-to-side and front-to-back. They bear real weight. Think table legs.

Stabilizer jacks extend down to touch the ground and cut bounce. They don't lift anything. More like kicking a wobbly chair leg into place—not holding you up, just keeping things steady.

💡 Golden Rule

Never use stabilizer jacks to level your RV. They're not built for it. Bending your frame this way is a $3,000+ mistake.

Types of Leveling Jacks

1. Scissor Jacks (Manual)

Price: $25–60 each | Best for: Travel trailers, pop-ups, budget setups

Crank-handle jacks that come stock on most travel trailers. They work, they're cheap, and they're slow. Cranking all four takes 10–15 minutes if you're moving fast.

Fine for weekend trips. But if you're setting up every other day, the cranking gets old real quick.

2. Electric Stabilizer Jacks

Price: $200–500 per pair | Best for: Travel trailers, 5th wheels with electric prep

Push a button and they extend. Thirty seconds instead of fifteen minutes. Most new mid-range trailers come with these on the rear, though the front often still uses a manual tongue jack.

Worth the money if you move often. My buddy swapped his manual scissors for electric and called it the best upgrade in three years of RVing.

3. Hydraulic Leveling Systems

Price: $2,000–5,000+ | Best for: Class A, Class C motorhomes, high-end 5th wheels

One-button auto-leveling. The system senses the angle and extends all four jacks automatically. The setup you want if you're driving a 40-foot diesel pusher.

Lippert Ground Control and Power Gear are the two big names. Both work well. Lippert gets the edge for app control—level from your phone while standing outside watching.

Leveling Blocks: The Unsung Hero

Jacks can only do so much on a slope. Leveling blocks are what you drive onto before dropping the jacks.

The Step-by-Step Leveling Process

How I level my 28-foot travel trailer every time:

  1. Check side-to-side first. Bubble level on the floor. If it's off, drive onto leveling blocks on the low side.
  2. Chock the wheels. Always. No exceptions. X-Chocks between the tires give you double security.
  3. Uncouple the hitch (for travel trailers). Drop the tongue jack to level front-to-back.
  4. Extend stabilizer jacks until they just touch the ground. Snug, not lifting. Put Lynx blocks under them too.
  5. Walk test. Walk around inside. Still bouncing? Tighten stabilizers a quarter turn.

💡 Pro Tip

Put a block of wood or Lynx under each stabilizer jack. Less extension = less wobble. The further a jack extends, the more it flexes. Keep 'em short and solid.

Extra Stability Tricks

Still bouncing after leveling? (Common with longer trailers.) Try these:

StrongArm Stabilizers — $150–200 per set. Cross-braces connecting scissor jacks to the frame. Cuts bounce by roughly 80%. The single best upgrade for travel trailer stability.

Kingpin Tripod Stabilizer — $80–120. For 5th wheels. Supports the kingpin to reduce front-end wobble. Easy setup, surprisingly effective.

X-Chock Wheel Stabilizers — $50–70 per pair. Expand between your tires to stop movement. Use every time, even on flat sites.
